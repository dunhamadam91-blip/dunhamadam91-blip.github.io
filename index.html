    Research Interests

    Abhinav's research interests are in development economics, labour economics and the economics of innovation. His current work studies how mobile internet access changes the way people work in rural India, how new farming technologies spread through village networks, and how public research funding shapes local job markets. He combines large household surveys, administrative data and field experiments to answer these questions.

    He has presented his work at the Canadian Economics Association Annual Meeting and the Northeast Universities Development Consortium (NEUDC). See the Research page for papers and abstracts.

    Research

    Job Market Paper

    Connected and Informal: Mobile Internet and the Reallocation of Labour in Rural India

    Sole-authored, 2026

    Paper (PDF)
    This paper studies how the rollout of 4G mobile networks across Indian villages affected where and how people work. Using the staggered timing of tower construction and household survey data from 2014 to 2022, I find that mobile internet access moved workers out of farm labour and into non-farm self-employment, raising household earnings by about 8 percent. The gains are concentrated among young and literate workers, while older workers see little change.

    Working Papers

    Learning from Neighbours: Social Networks and the Adoption of Drought-Resistant Seeds

    with Firstname Lastname, 2025

    Paper (PDF)
    We run a field experiment with 1,200 farmers in Maharashtra to study how information about a new seed variety spreads through village networks. Farmers whose close contacts were trained were 15 percentage points more likely to adopt, but only when those contacts farmed land of a similar size.

    Public Research Funding and Local Labour Markets: Evidence from Canadian Universities

    with Firstname Lastname and Firstname Lastname, 2025

    Paper (PDF)
    We link federal research grants to firm and employment records to measure how university research spending affects nearby high-skill jobs. A one million dollar increase in grants creates about six new jobs within 50 km over five years, mostly in young firms.
